About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the School of Water Resources and Hydropower Engineering, Wuhan University, supervised by Prof. Jiyun Song. My research focuses on urban climate dynamics, heat extremes, and land–atmosphere interactions, with a particular interest in developing physics-informed deep learning approaches for high-resolution urban weather modeling and nowcasting.

My work aims to bridge physical simulations (WRF/urban canopy models) with spatiotemporal deep learning to resolve urban thermal environments at meter scales, and to translate these insights into climate-just planning and operations for heat-resilient cities.

Research & Project Experience

PhD Research: Physics-Informed Deep Learning for Urban Weather Nowcasting and Emulation

2024 – Now

Wuhan University, School of Water Resources and Hydropower Engineering

  • Causal ML for urban expansion–heatwave nexus. Used double machine learning to decouple urbanization-induced thermal penalties from climate variability across 1,690 Chinese cities (1990–2022); revealed a dichotomy between internal 3D densification (local intensity/onset) and intercity coalescence (regional frequency/duration), and identified a ~50 km spatial threshold for mega urban heat dome formation. Under review at Science Advances.
  • HyperClim-Former: 5-metre thermal stress mapping and climate-just routing. Developed a physics-guided deep learning framework coupling mesoscale forcings with 3D urban morphology for 5 m, 10-min UTCI reconstruction (RMSE = 1.00 °C); integrated with agent-based courier simulations and multi-objective Pareto optimization to reduce heat stress by 21.1% with only 4.3 min added delay per shift. Submitted to npj Urban Sustainability.
  • Physics-informed STGNN for station-to-grid urban weather nowcasting. Designed five physically motivated graph topologies (proximity, thermal similarity, wind advection, terrain, LCZ) for 333 m station-to-grid downscaling; implemented WRF-to-observation transfer learning with thermodynamic constraints, reducing WRF biases by 25–67% while retaining 92.7% spatial heterogeneity. Submitted to Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics.
